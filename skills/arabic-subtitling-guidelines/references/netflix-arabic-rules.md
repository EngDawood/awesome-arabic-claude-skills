## 1. Arabic language style
- MSA only, no dialect. Closest-meaning MSA word when no exact equivalent exists.
- Avoid dialectal expressions (e.g. برجاء، يا خبر، يا ستّار).
- This Arabic guide overrides general language instructions elsewhere.
- End subtitles with relevant punctuation where appropriate.
- Translate place names/currencies to Arabic forms: المكسيك، أثينا، البيرو، بروكسيل، اليورو، البيزو.

## 2. Translation and transliteration
- Prioritize clarity, accuracy, naturalness.
- Research an established Arabic equivalent first.
- If no equivalent, or it's ambiguous/rare, transliterate in double quotes.
- If the transliteration is already common usage and pluralizable (راديو/راديوهات), quotes aren't needed.

## 3. Acronyms and abbreviations
- Translate acronyms with a well-known Arabic equivalent (CIA -> الوكالة المركزية للاستخبارات).
- Some are transliterated as spoken (OPEC -> أوبك, UNICEF -> يونيسف).
- Acronyms without a known equivalent: transliterate without periods, in quotes, optionally with a short descriptive gloss ("NBC" -> "محطة "إن بي سي").
- Common acronym/abbreviation forms may be used once the full name is spelled out at the start of the asset (إن بي إيه for NBA, سي آي إيه for CIA).
- Do not invent Arabic abbreviations that mimic English ones.
- Prefer full words (مساءً، متر، غرام) but accepted Arabic abbreviations are fine (ص for صباحًا, ملم, كغ).
- No periods in SI unit abbreviations; space between number and unit.

## 4. Character limitation
- 42 characters per line.

## 5. Character names
- Transliterate; don't translate unless Netflix supplies an approved translation.
- Arabic order is first name/last name (opposite of e.g. Korean last/first): Park Shin-Hye -> شن هي بارك أو شنهي بارك.
- Transliterate nicknames; translate only if the meaning is relevant to viewers.
- Historical/mythical figures: translate per context (Santa Claus -> بابا نويل or سانتا كلوز).
- Political figures/celebrities: use well-known transliterations/translations (MBS -> محمد بن سلمان, not transliterated).

## 6. Continuity (ellipses)
- Ellipsis = single character U+2026, never three periods.
- Do not use ellipsis/dash when a sentence simply continues across two subtitles.
- Use ellipsis for an intentional pause of >=2 s, accidental suspension, or abrupt interruption; if the sentence continues in the next subtitle, don't start that one with an ellipsis — move the conjunction there instead.
- Use ellipsis without a space to show a subtitle starting mid-sentence.
- A sentence interrupted by a second speaker (or by an FN or SDH cue) needs ellipses before and after the interruption.
- Incomplete speech ends with an ellipsis.
- Avoid ellipses within one subtitle unless they add real readability/meaning value.
- Cut off a word mid-syllable at a natural break, not mid-letter, when the source cuts a word.
- A word abandoned mid-word (character changes their mind) still gets an ellipsis: يا لك من وقـ… أمرك يا سيدي.

## 7. Documentary/unscripted
- Translate a speaker's title only the first time they appear.
- Interrupting dialogue with a speaker's title: ellipsis at the end of the preceding subtitle and the start of the next.
- Subtitle TV/movie clip dialogue only if plot-pertinent and rights granted.
- News tickers/banners from archive clips: no subtitles unless plot-pertinent.

## 8. Dual speakers
- Hyphen + space at the start of each line, max one speaker per line.
- Each line should be a self-contained sentence where possible.

## 9. Font
- Arial-style proportional sans-serif placeholder, size relative to video resolution (must fit 42 chars), white.

## 10. On-screen text (forced narratives)
- Include only if plot-pertinent.
- When on-screen text and dialogue overlap, the more plot-pertinent one wins; don't over-truncate or tank reading speed to fit both.
- FN duration should mimic the on-screen text's duration, except where reading speed/surrounding dialogue takes precedence.
- Delete redundant FNs (identical to dialogue or on-screen text already covered).
- FNs in straight double quotes (" "), except for foreign dialogue.
- Never combine an FN with dialogue in one subtitle; if both need the same slot, dialogue wins.
- If an FN interrupts dialogue, use ellipses at the end of the preceding subtitle and start of the next (per section 6).
- Multi-subtitle FN: one set of quotes per applicable subtitle (open+close each one).

## 11. Foreign dialogue and words
- Translate foreign dialogue only if the viewer was meant to understand it (i.e. it was subtitled in the template).
- Famous-place nicknames get the direct Arabic translation of the real name (Big Apple -> نيويورك), not the nickname.

## 12. Italics
- Never use italics in Arabic.

## 13. Line treatment and line breaks
- Max 2 lines; prefer 1 line unless it exceeds the char limit.
- Avoid a lone word on line 2 — shorten or break earlier instead.
- Prefer bottom-heavy pyramid shape.
- Two separate subjects from the same speaker may be split into two lines for clarity.
- Never break a line between:
  - فعل وفاعل (verb/subject)
  - حرف ملازم للفعل وفعله (particle bound to its verb)
  - صفة ونعت (adjective and described noun)
  - مضاف ومضاف إليه (construct/possessive pair)
  - حرف استثناء واستثناء (exception particle and excepted noun)
  - حرف نداء ومنادى (vocative particle and vocative)
  - حرف جر واسم مجرور (preposition and its noun)
  - عدد وتمييز (number and specified noun)

## 14. Quotation marks
- Quotes go at the start and end of the whole quoted passage, not around every subtitle within it — even across multiple subtitles by the same speaker reading/quoting.
- Use quotes when a character is shown reading aloud.
- Straight double quotes " " with no inner spaces for regular quotations.
- Parentheses ( ) for quotes-within-quotes or names within a quote.
- Punctuation goes inside the quote marks if the quoted text is an independent clause, outside if not.
- Direct speech may be introduced by a colon or comma — pick one and stay consistent.
- Double quotes around proper names of people and places (when quoted per style, e.g. transliterated foreign names/brand names).
- Add a kashida (U+0640) to ال before it meets a quote/bracket: "الـ"برونكس (not "ال"برونكس).
- Keep ال outside the quotation marks themselves.
- Air quotes on screen -> apply quotation marks to the equivalent Arabic word/phrase.

## 15. Reading speed limits
- Adult programs: up to 20 characters/second.
- Children's programs: up to 17 characters/second.
- (SDH limits are higher — see section 24.)

## 16. Hashtags, email, website names
- "hashtag" -> وسم or هاشتاغ (transliterated); text after # may be transliterated, translated, or kept with the # symbol, linguist's judgment. Example: #HumanRights -> وسم حقوق الإنسان / هاشتاغ حقوق الإنسان / #حقوق الإنسان.
- On-screen email/website: "...على الموقع/البريد الإلكتروني الظاهر على الشاشة".
- Spoken email/website: transliterate and quote, e.g. "نتفليكس دوت كوم".
- Never use Latin letters in Arabic subtitles for an email or website.

## 17. Repetitions
- Don't translate a word/phrase the same speaker repeats more than once.
- If repeated twice in a row, time the subtitle to the audio but translate once only.
- Don't drop repetitions the visuals depend on (e.g. pointing at multiple things).

## 18. Songs and poetry
- Subtitle only plot-pertinent songs with rights granted.
- Opening/ending theme songs: subtitle only if plot-pertinent or Netflix-instructed; normally skip for adult content (except SDH).
- Ellipsis when a song continues in the background but is no longer subtitled.
- Punctuation: only ? and ! allowed.
- Double quotes for song lyrics and titles; quotes needed only at the very start/end of a song (or of an interrupted portion).
- No italics.
- Quote a recited poem, whether someone else's or the character's own (their own poem/song/book/prior dialogue still counts as a quotation).
- Existing published translations of poetry/literary works: only usable if public domain or rights cleared.

## 19. Titles
- Main titles: subtitle on-screen branded titles when an approved Arabic title exists in KNP/Terminology and differs from the on-screen card; always use the approved title, don't retranslate.
- Skip subtitling if the on-screen title and approved Arabic title are identical.
- Subtitle when the approved title partially transliterates/translates/transcreates and doesn't fully match on-screen text (e.g. Minecraft: Story Mode -> Minecraft: وضع القصة).
- If the approved translation can't fit the line/char limits, the limits may be exceeded rather than the translation split across events.
- Never italicize the main title event.
- Episode titles: subtitle only if on-screen or voiced-over; use the approved KNP translation.
- Titles of existing works: use official/well-known translations; transliterate if none exist.

## 20. Numbers
- 1–10 written out in full letters, observing Arabic number/counted-noun agreement, except for time/date references. E.g. رأيت رجلين اثنين، اشتريت كتابًا واحدًا فقط، عشرة أطفال، خمس سيدات.
- Above 10, numerals: 11, 12, 65, 125, 2,365.
- These rules may bend for space, reading speed, list consistency, or figures of speech.
- Start a sentence with a numeral only if it improves readability.
- Currencies spelled out fully, no abbreviations (دولار أمريكي, not د.أ).
- Ordinals 1–9 spelled out (الموسم الأول, not الموسم 1).
- Ordinals from double digits up: numerals with kashida, e.g. القرن الـ15, الـ21.
- 4+ digit numbers: comma thousands separator (1,234), no space; no comma for years (1940).
- Decimal separator "."; never drop the leading 0 (0.123, not .123).
- Time: 12-hour format with Arabic day-part terms. Dates follow Arabic norms, don't mimic English comma placement.
- Mid-sentence date: في 21 يناير / في الـ21 من يناير, or مساء 19 ديسمبر / مساء الـ19 من ديسمبر — pick whichever reads best.
- Percentages spelled out for correct sentence grammar (20% -> عشرين بالمئة, in correct grammatical form).
- Measurements converted to metric unless the original unit is plot-relevant.

## 21. Punctuation
- No space before commas, question marks, or exclamation marks.
- Never combine ? and ! (no ?! or !?) — pick the one that fits the intonation/meaning.
- Don't substitute a comma for a repeated conjunction in a list: المدونون والمترجمون والمترجمون الشفويون (not المدونون، المترجمون والمترجمون الشفويون).

## 22. Diacritics (الحركات)
- Use only where their absence changes meaning.
- Tanween al-fath: placed on the letter before the alef (Netflix-specific rendering choice, avoids line-height overlap).
- Shaddah — use to distinguish:
  - verb vs. noun vs. particle: شابَ (his hair whitened) vs. شابّ (young man)
  - similarly-written letter pairs: ألّا vs. وألا; هل (interrogative) vs. هلّ (appeared)
  - heavy confirmation nun / feminine plural nun: لنشاهدنّ، حياتهنّ، هنّ
  - 1st-person-singular/dual ya: إليّ، والديّ
- Shaddah — don't use for:
  - nisba ya: عربي، أفريقي، علمي
  - solar letters (except cases above): الشمس، الدرب
- Dammah — mark to disambiguate minimal pairs: سيدة مُحبة؛ بعد/بُعد؛ عشر/عُشر؛ أخرج/أُخرج؛ أظهر/أُظهر; and for passive-voice verbs: يُكتب.

## 23. Special instructions
- Render dialogue (including expletives) faithfully — no dialect substitution, no added or removed obscenity level.
- Match tone, register, class, formality of the original.
- Plot-pertinent dialogue takes precedence over background dialogue.
- Don't reproduce deliberate mispronunciations/misspellings unless plot-pertinent.
- Family terms: translate by actual relationship context (Aunt -> خالة or عمّة; Uncle -> عمّ or خال).
- Months follow the Gregorian calendar names (أغسطس, not آب).
- Don't translate onomatopoeia (wow, ouch).
- Don't translate filler words (just, really, basically, you know) unless they carry meaning.

## 24. SDH (Subtitles for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing)
- Include as much of the original content as possible; don't water down dialogue.
- Dubbed content: base the SDH on the dubbing script/audio, matching as closely as reading speed allows.
- Reading speed: adults up to 23 cps, children up to 20 cps.
- Truncate original dialogue only where reading speed/sync genuinely require it.
- TV/movie clips: transcribe all audible lines if possible; if audio conflicts, prioritize the most plot-pertinent.
- Subtitle same-language audible songs that don't interfere with dialogue.
- Song title identifiers in quotes: ["أغنية "فوريفر يور غيرل].
- Lyrics: music note ♪ at start and end of each subtitle, with a space to surrounding text; duets get a note on each speaker's line.
- Brackets [ ] for speaker IDs/sound effects.
- Identifiers/sound effects lowercase except proper nouns.
- Use speaker IDs/sound effects only when not visually identifiable.
- Unidentified characters: [امرأة] / [رجل] / [صوت رجل] / [صوت امرأة] (don't reveal info the narrative hasn't yet).
- Gender-neutral identifiers follow the voice heard: [طبيب]/[طبيبة]/[بائع]/[بائعة].
- Repeated same-identifier speakers in sequence: [رجل 2] etc.
- Ambient music generic ID: ["موسيقى "روك], [موسيقى "جاز" هادئة عبر المذياع].
- Include plot-pertinent sound effects unless the visuals already convey them.
- Conjugated verb form for actions: [تصرخ/يصرخ].
- Subtitle plot-pertinent silence (e.g. music cutting abruptly).
- Be descriptive: adverbs for speed/volume/tone of sounds and voices; describe audio, not visuals/actions.
- Sound cue nouns stay indefinite, no definite article: [زقزقة عصافير], [خرير مياه].
- Sound effects interrupting dialogue: place the bracketed cue as its own subtitle between the two dialogue parts.
- Speaker ID and its dialogue share the same subtitle line.
- Foreign dialogue in SDH: [بالإسبانية] if translated and meant to be understood; [يتكلم الإسبانية]/[تتكلم الإسبانية] if not meant to be understood (match gender/conjugation); always verify the actual language — never the generic [يتكلم لغة أجنبية].
- Proper names in SDH keep double quotes around the name: ["بيونسيه"], not [بيونسيه].

## 25. SDH — dialect/variant Arabic content
- Applies to Arabic-language content only.
- SDH identifiers are always MSA, in both dialect and MSA SDH files — never write identifiers in dialect.
- In dialect SDH files, spell words the MSA way even when pronounced differently in the dialect audio.
- See the Arabic Dialects Playbook (not fetched here) for more.
- Foreign words/lines within Arabic dialogue: translate into the target language (MSA or the Arabic variant) with language identifiers.
- Flag a language switch with SDH identifiers only if plot-pertinent; if the switch happens mid-sentence, stack identifiers at the start rather than splitting mid-line.
- Onomatopoeia stays in the original language or is reproduced in Arabic SDH, in its concise form even if the audio exaggerates it (آه! واو!, not آآآآه! واووو!).
- Swear words spoken in Arabic: transcribe accurately and true to source in the dialect/variant SDH file.
- Swear words spoken in a foreign language: translate as truly to source as possible into the dialect/variant SDH file.

## 26. Arabic templates (source-language authoring)
- Applies to Arabic-language content only.
- Foreign dialogue within Arabic audio: translate into the target language with annotations flagging which part was foreign and in what language; never transliterate the foreign words into Arabic letters.
- Template authors must annotate foreign-dialogue spans so SDH can label them correctly, e.g. noting "Sorry - آسف in the first line is in English."
- Same profanity-in-Arabic / profanity-in-foreign-language rules as SDH (sections 24–25) apply here.

## 27. Special considerations for FNs in Arabic content
- Only cover foreign dialogue in FNs when a full sentence is spoken in a foreign language — not single words ("Hello", "Bye", "Sorry").
- On-screen Franco-Arabic text (Arabic in Latin letters) must be added to Arabic FN files.
- Foreign-language on-screen texting must be included in Arabic FNs; if it's in a dialect other than the show's main dialect, use best judgment.
- Non-plot-pertinent songs: don't include in Arabic FNs. For dialect songs differing from the show's spoken dialect, use judgment on whether to add an MSA lyric translation (in MSA files).

## 28. Profanity and swear words
- Never censor dialogue (see General Requirements).
- For detailed MSA profanity guidance, consult Netflix's dedicated profanity guidelines (not fetched here — link from the Arabic TTSG page).

## 29. References Netflix points to
AlMaany Dictionary, an Arabic conjugator, Arabic grammar references, a diacritics tool, Arabic punctuation guides, Doha Historical Dictionary, Al Baheth. "Always use best judgment; cross-check multiple sources."

# General Requirements (all languages)

## Duration
- Min 5/6 s per event (20 frames @ 24 fps). Max 7 s per event.

## File format / glyphs
- Use current Netflix delivery spec for file format/type. Only characters in the Netflix Accepted Glyph List (not fetched here) may be used.

## Line treatment
- 2 lines max; 1 line preferred unless over the char limit.
- Break after punctuation, before conjunctions, before prepositions (general/English-oriented rule — Arabic's own line-break restrictions in section 13 above take precedence for Arabic).
- Never separate: noun+article, noun+adjective, first+last name, verb+subject pronoun, prepositional verb+preposition, verb+auxiliary/reflexive/negation.

## Positioning
- Center-justified, top or bottom of screen (Japanese allows vertical). Avoid overlapping on-screen text; if unavoidable, place wherever reads easier.

## Consistency
- Use KNPs/formality tables across episodes/seasons; agree workflow with the Netflix contact.

## Netflix credits
- Don't subtitle Netflix's own credits unless instructed; if requested, follow the Originals Credit Translation doc and the language's FN rules.

## Main titles/dedications
- Subtitle the main title per the language TTSG.
- Subtitle plot-pertinent on-screen text not covered in dialogue (e.g. "Based on True Events", "In Loving Memory of Jane").

## Currency
- Never convert currency values in the file; keep money amounts in the original currency.

## Brand names
- Use the same brand name if well known locally; or the locally-known name for that brand; or a generic term. Never swap in a different company's trademark.

## Quotations (of existing texts)
- Prefer originating a new translation to avoid rights issues. An existing translation may be reused only if public domain (100-year rule of thumb) or rights/payment secured.

## Translator credits
- Add as the last event of the file, using the approved Original Credits translation doc wording.
- Credit translators, not companies; only for episodic/feature content, not marketing/supplemental assets.
- Entirely in the target language; timed for reading speed, up to 5 s; not on-screen simultaneously with the Netflix ident.
- For SDH: credit only when translating from the original language, not when transcribing.
- May be omitted only with a formal rights waiver from the translator.
- Multiple translators may share one credit line (e.g. "Subtitle translation by: Will Byers, Jane Hopper").

## Technical (TTML)
- Percentage values only, never pixels. tts:textAlign/tts:displayAlign for alignment; static tts:extent/tts:origin. tts:fontSize = 100%.

# Subtitle Timing Guidelines (all languages, 24fps baseline; scale "half a second" to other frame rates: 15 frames@30fps, 30 frames@60fps; min gap stays 2 frames at any rate)

## Principles
- Sync to image and audio; sit neatly within shots; avoid spoilers (don't reveal a punchline/plot point early if there's a visible on-screen reaction).

## 1. Timing to audio
- In-time on the first frame of audio, or within 1–2 frames.
- If no subtitle follows, extend out-time to about half a second past audio end rather than cutting exactly at dialogue end.
- If extending conflicts with a shot change, prefer syncing the out-time to the shot change (edit text for reading speed if needed) — unless the dialogue itself crosses the shot change.
- Last resort: out-time up to 12 frames after the shot change if no lingering effect results.
- Chain/close gaps under half a second between consecutive subtitles by bumping the previous out-time to 2 frames before the next in-time.

## 2. Timing to shot changes
- Dialogue starting on or within half a second after a shot change: in-time = first frame of the shot change.
- Out-time within half a second of the last frame before a shot change: extend to the shot change (respecting the 2-frame gap).
- If dialogue ends before a shot change with nothing following, still set out-time to 2 frames before the shot change.
- Apply judgment if a subtitle would otherwise linger too long on screen.

## 3. Minimum duration
- Never shorter than 20 frames (~4/5 s — treat as equivalent to the 5/6 s in General Requirements). Reserve the bare minimum for 1–2 word subtitles; extend longer subtitles when possible.

## 4. Dialogue crossing shot changes
- Allowed only when the dialogue itself crosses the shot change.
- In-time: bring forward to half a second before the shot change (if dialogue starts 9–11 frames before it at 24fps) or move to the first frame of the new shot.
- Out-time: 2 frames before the shot change, or at least half a second after it, if reading speed allows.
- Otherwise, follow the normal timing-to-audio rules.
- Never cross a scene change except when a speaker starts before it and the dialogue continues into the next scene.

## 5. Gaps between subtitles
- Minimum 2 frames, any frame rate.
- At 24 fps, gaps of 3–11 frames must be closed to 2 frames; a gap must be either 2 frames or >= half a second, nothing in between.
- Close gaps by extending the previous out-time — respecting shot-change and audio-sync rules.
- If the previous out-time is already extended half a second past audio, don't extend further to close gaps; consider re-segmenting or merging instead.

## 6. Borrowing time
- Merge with a neighboring subtitle to fix uneven reading speed when needed; keep dual-speaker formatting correct after merging; may require re-segmenting/re-timing neighbors too.

## 7. Forced narratives for on-screen text
- Mirror the on-screen text's timing as closely as the min/max duration rules allow.
- For fading text, set in/out roughly halfway through the fade; watch back to confirm sync.
- If on-screen text persists for a whole shot, still end the FN 2 frames before the shot change.
- These FN timing rules override the standard dialogue timing-to-shot/audio rules.
- FNs may come out early if dialogue is present and takes precedence.

# Subtitle Templates (source-authoring notes — relevant when the Arabic file is itself the template/source, or when translating from an English template)

- A template = an edited, positioned, researched, annotated, checked file timed to shot and audio; not expected to be verbatim.
- Templates should follow this guide plus the TTSG of the template's own language.

## Coverage
- All plot-pertinent dialogue in any language (foreign dialogue only if meant to be understood).
- Don't subtitle repeated words/phrases by the same speaker (see Repetitions above).
- Reading-speed editing strategies: deletion, reformulation, re-timing, re-segmentation, merging, contractions, dropping already-established proper nouns, dropping unnecessary repetitions, omitting unimportant paralinguistic filler. Avoid unnecessary condensing; never paraphrase for its own sake.
- All plot-pertinent on-screen events/dedications, tagged as FNs.
- Documentaries: on-screen speaker ID text (name/company/title) included the first time a speaker appears — not redundant for non-Latin scripts.

## Annotations
- Required in every template, in English, explaining: cultural references, slang, idioms, jokes, complex grammar, puns, sarcasm/irony, register, formality/class, tone, intent, character relationships, names/nicknames, age, spatial relationships, cultural nuance (race, religion, geopolitics, ethnicity, LGBT+), and offensiveness level where a translation choice softened a trigger word (especially in trailers/supplementals, which must not be more offensive than "living room safe").
- No annotation needed for terms already in the nominated dictionary or to justify timing/text-reduction choices.

## Formatting specific to templates
- Capitalization: dialogue and dialogue FNs use sentence case; on-screen-text FNs use ALL CAPS except long passages (prologues/epilogues), which use sentence case.
- Brackets: avoid unless format requires them (e.g. phone area code); square brackets reserved for SDH sound/speaker labels.
- 42 characters/line for most Latin-alphabet languages — defer to the language-specific TTSG (Arabic: also 42).
- Reading speed for English templates: adults up to 17 cps, children up to 15 cps; non-English templates match their own language's interlingual subtitle limits (Arabic: 20/17 as above).
- Segmentation: split only at clause boundaries; keep each subtitle logically self-contained.

## Pivot templates (bridging two non-adjacent languages)
- Keep the pivot as a linguistically fluent, non-transcreated back-translation of the true source.
- Never localize cultural references/names/locations — keep as in the source audio, explained via annotation instead.
- Provide literal translations/explanations of idioms/wordplay lost in the pivot.
- Don't change plot points. Don't convert measurements/currencies/dates/times.
- Extra annotation is expected for anything the pivot language can't carry: formality, gender, negation, definiteness, grammatical mood/case, legal/technical terms, citations, and everything in the general annotation list above.

# Product Supplemental & Marketing Assets

- For technical aspects (duration, timing, frame gaps, positioning, Netflix credits) and formatting/translation rules, defer to the language-specific TTSG (this file) plus General Requirements.
- Marketing-asset-specific rule: always align subtitle text to match the original or dubbed audio in both language and timing — don't diverge from the audio the way a longform translation sometimes can.
- Follow the timing guidelines closely; these assets are often short and timing errors are more visible.
